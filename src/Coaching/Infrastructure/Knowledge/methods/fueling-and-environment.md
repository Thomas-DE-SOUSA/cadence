# Fuelling, hydration and environment

## Everyday fuelling
- Carbohydrate availability drives quality sessions; don't run hard chronically under-fuelled.
- Protein spread across the day supports repair; don't neglect it in endurance athletes.
- Hydrate to thirst across the day; dark urine is a rough under-hydration cue.

## Around sessions
- Easy runs under ~60–75 min generally need no in-run fuel.
- Long runs and race-pace work over ~75–90 min benefit from carbohydrate during the run;
  rehearse race fuelling in training, never try something new on race day.
- Post-hard-session: carbohydrate + protein within a couple of hours aids recovery.

## Environment
- **Heat/humidity** — expect slower paces; run by effort, hydrate more, add electrolytes on
  long/hot efforts. Adjust or move intensity if it's dangerous.
- **Cold** — warm up longer; the first km can feel harder.
- **Wind & hills** — judge by effort, not pace; into a headwind or uphill the same effort is
  slower and that's fine.
- **Altitude** — paces slow at height; ease intensity on arrival.

Coaching stance: when conditions are hard, prioritise the *purpose* of the session (aerobic
stimulus, threshold effort, etc.) over hitting an exact pace number.
