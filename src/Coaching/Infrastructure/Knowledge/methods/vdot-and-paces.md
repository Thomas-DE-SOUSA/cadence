# VDOT and training paces (Daniels framework)

VDOT is a single fitness score derived from a recent race/time-trial. From it we set five
training-pace zones. Cadence computes the athlete's VDOT and paces automatically — use the
provided values, do not invent paces.

## The five zones (purpose, not just pace)
- **E — Easy (~59–74% VO₂max):** the aerobic base. Builds capillaries, mitochondria,
  heart. Comfortable, conversational. Most weekly volume lives here. Long runs are E.
- **M — Marathon:** steady goal-marathon effort; endurance and fuel efficiency.
- **T — Threshold (~"comfortably hard", ~1-hour race effort):** raises lactate threshold —
  the single biggest lever for 10 km–marathon. Tempo runs and cruise intervals.
- **I — Interval (~95–100% VO₂max, work bouts ~3–5 min):** maximises VO₂max. Hard.
- **R — Repetition (faster than I, short bouts w/ full recovery):** speed, running economy,
  neuromuscular. Not about aerobic stress.

## Coaching rules of thumb
- Improvement usually comes from **more T work and adequate E volume**, not from making easy
  days moderate.
- One, at most two, genuine quality sessions per week for most amateurs; the rest E.
- Paces are guidance, not law: heat, hills, fatigue and altitude all justify running by
  effort rather than the exact number.
- A jump of several VDOT points is months of work; a realistic near-term target is usually
  1–2 points. If the goal implies a large VDOT gap before the race date, say so and set an
  intermediate target.
