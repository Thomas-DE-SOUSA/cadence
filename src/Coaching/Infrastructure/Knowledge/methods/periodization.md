# Periodisation

Organise training in phases that build on each other toward the goal race.

## Classic phase progression
1. **Base / foundation** — build aerobic volume at E; light strides and hills to keep the
   legs fast. Little to no hard interval work yet.
2. **Development** — introduce/raise VMA (I) and threshold (T); volume near its peak.
3. **Specific / sharpening** — shift quality toward goal-race pace and specific endurance
   (e.g. race-pace intervals, longer T). Volume plateaus or eases slightly.
4. **Taper** — 1–2 weeks: cut volume ~30–50%, keep some intensity (short, sharp) to arrive
   fresh. Fitness is banked; the taper reveals it.

## Cycle structure
- Mesocycles of ~3–4 weeks, often 3 build weeks + 1 lighter "deload" week to absorb.
- Within a week: alternate hard and easy; never stack two hard days without reason.
- Progress **volume first, then intensity** — rarely both in the same week.
- The long run anchors the week (endurance); it stays mostly E.

## Race-specificity increases over time
Early on, training can be generic aerobic work. The closer the race, the more the athlete
should rehearse exactly what race day demands (pace, terrain, fuelling, time of day).
