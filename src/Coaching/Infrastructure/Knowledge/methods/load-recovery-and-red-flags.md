# Load management, recovery, and red flags

## Recovery is where fitness is made
Hard training is only a stimulus; adaptation happens during recovery. Under-recovery →
stagnation, then overreaching, then injury/illness. When in doubt, recover.

## Managing load
- **Ramp gradually** — avoid large week-to-week jumps in volume or intensity. A useful lens
  is the acute:chronic ratio (this week's load vs the recent 3–4 week average); spikes are
  the main injury risk. Prefer steady progression with regular lighter weeks.
- Deload every 3–4 weeks (reduce volume ~20–40%) to consolidate.
- Sleep and fuelling are training inputs; poor sleep or under-eating blunt adaptation.

## Reading fatigue and soreness
- **DOMS / general muscular fatigue** after a hard or unaccustomed session is normal. The
  answer is easy running, mobility, or rest — not more intensity. It typically eases in
  24–72 h. Even "easy" pace feeling hard is a signal to back off, not push.
- Persistent heavy legs, rising resting HR, disturbed sleep, low mood/motivation across
  several days can signal accumulated fatigue → insert extra easy/rest.

## Red flags — stop and screen (refer to a professional)
- Sharp, stabbing, or localised pain (esp. bone, joint, tendon).
- Pain that worsens during the run or changes your gait.
- Swelling, pain at rest or at night, pain that persists day to day.
- Any chest pain, unusual breathlessness, dizziness or fainting.
Never coach through these; advise rest and medical/physio assessment.

## Adapting a single day (decision ladder)
Keep → shorten → drop the intensity (hard→easy) → swap with a nearby day → cross-train →
full rest. Choose the least disruptive option that respects the signal, and protect the
key sessions of the week when possible.
